# Dunlo: measurement and evaluation

The evaluation will examine whether discovery leads into continued useful Khan practice and independent application.

## Keep the measures distinct

- Discovery: items explored, saved interests and interaction choices.
- Khan access: specific resources opened. An open is not completion.
- Learner reports: completed, still difficult or unable to access.
- Authorized Khan records: activity available through a permitted reporting route, when accessible.
- Independent application: fresh questions answered without the earlier guide or answer.
- Later recall: unfamiliar follow-up questions after a defined delay, such as 7–14 days.

The present local history serves the learner. A consented evaluation must establish its own participant keys, collection method, permissions and retention policy before collecting study data. Public visitors are not automatically research participants.

## Design

Start with voluntary usability and access feedback. A later structured evaluation can use unfamiliar immediate tasks and follow-ups. Report the full eligible denominator, non-completion, assistance and access failures. A feasible comparison with direct Khan use would help investigate the contribution of Dunlo; an uncontrolled pre/post gain alone does not establish causality.

Recruitment counts and partners will be set only when practical access and permissions exist. Do not reuse the earlier fixed 80–120 learner assumption as a commitment.

## Reporting

Report reach, repeated participation, relevant Khan activity, independent task performance, delayed follow-up and operating costs separately. Engineering tests validate software behavior, not educational effectiveness. Include what did not work and which content or access conditions need improvement.
