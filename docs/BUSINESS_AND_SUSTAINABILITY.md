# BACKTRACK: business and sustainability

A learner needs help choosing which earlier step to review. A school needs a manageable way to connect that review to the current lesson. This plan sets out the resources needed to provide that routine and measure its value.

## Value proposition

For learners, BACKTRACK aims to reduce uncertainty about what to review and preserve knowledge they can demonstrate. For schools, it aims to turn existing Khan access into a repeatable recovery routine with manageable teacher effort. For a sponsor, the proposed purchase is a supported implementation and evidence package, not a count of registrations.

Core learner access remains free. There is no validated institutional price, paying customer, secured sponsor, or revenue claim. The first business task is to measure the resources required to run a useful school cycle.

## Planning allocation: PHP 100,000

This scenario uses the champion implementation-grant amount. It excludes the separate team award and national-competition travel. These are allocation ceilings, not supplier quotations or secured funds. The institution and organizer must approve actual grant expenses.

- Learning design and independent mathematics review: PHP 24,000.
- Connectivity and shared-device access support: PHP 18,000.
- School deployment and local travel: PHP 16,000.
- Assessment, evaluation, and data handling: PHP 18,000.
- Product operations, maintenance, and hosting: PHP 8,000.
- Teacher onboarding and replication materials: PHP 8,000.
- Short-form content and recruitment materials: PHP 3,000.
- Contingency: PHP 5,000.

**Total: PHP 100,000.** No paid model inference is required by the current product. Actual hosting, transport, printing, facilitator time, and educator-review costs require quotes or observed logs before commitment.

## Why this allocation changed

The inherited plan spent more on general product/tooling and acquisition. This plan protects educator review, access, and evaluation because a polished route without trustworthy mathematics or a feasible school routine cannot establish impact. Short-form content starts small and must demonstrate qualified route starts before additional spending.

Use existing school devices where permitted. Do not budget a token device purchase and imply it solves access for an entire cohort. If reliable access cannot be arranged within the allocation, reduce the cohort or use a smaller readiness study. Document who remains excluded.

## Grant scenarios

At PHP 100,000, plan for 80–120 learners across two or three cohorts, subject to capacity. At PHP 75,000, preserve assessment and permissions while narrowing reach, travel, and the number of destination cycles. At PHP 50,000, a 40–60 learner, two-cohort feasibility scenario may be more responsible. These are alternative planning scenarios, not commitments or effects of grant size on learning.

At no grant, preserve the public tool and conduct only institution-approved usability work that the team can support. Do not quietly finance an unfunded school deployment with teacher labor.

## Unit economics to measure

- Cash cost per enrolled learner: actual direct expenditure divided by all enrolled formal learners.
- Cash cost per active learner: actual direct expenditure divided by learners meeting the prespecified participation definition.
- Cost per documented retained reentry: actual direct expenditure divided by learners meeting the full post-and-delayed criterion. If none qualify, report no documented recoveries and the spent amount; do not divide by zero or replace it with registrations.
- Full implementation cost: cash plus separately valued in-kind staff, volunteer, facilities, and device time.
- Support hours per school and per repeated cycle.
- Authoring/review hours per validated destination kit, including revisions and maintenance.
- Acquisition cost per qualified route start, with organic labor valued separately from cash.

Illustration of arithmetic only: PHP 100,000 divided by 100 enrolled learners is PHP 1,000 per enrollee. It says nothing about activity or recovery. Do not present a recovery unit cost until its actual denominator exists.

## Continuation pathways

1. **University extension:** integrate destination-kit maintenance and school support into a supervised outreach program. Validate ownership, staff availability, successor teams, and an annual support allocation.
2. **School network or education nonprofit:** support facilitator training, access, and evaluation across several campuses. Test willingness to fund the service and the reporting burden.
3. **LGU or CSR sponsor:** support access and a documented cycle in a defined community. Keep procurement, reporting, and safeguarding responsibilities explicit.
4. **Khan ecosystem collaboration:** seek advice and supported reporting opportunities through official capacity building. Do not call this a partnership before an agreement exists.

Interview prospective budget owners before quoting a package price. Ask what they already fund, their budget cycle, decision authority, evidence threshold, allowable costs, and whether they would pay for recurring support after the grant. A letter of interest is different from an authorized budget.

## An actual decision after March

Continue in a school only if an educator owns the routine, a successor can maintain it, access remains feasible, and the evidence justifies the burden. Transfer the kit and maintenance documentation. Keep core practice free. If no payer or institutional owner emerges, release the reviewed material within applicable licenses and maintain only the scope the team can support honestly.

## Cost controls

Use monthly budget-to-actual reviews, receipts, expense purpose, approving adult, and grant-category codes. Separate team-award spending and grant spending. Preapprove any paid API or hosting upgrade. Track foreign-currency services at the actual charged exchange rate, not an invented permanent conversion.
