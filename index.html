The problem

One missed lesson doesn’t stay one missed lesson.

A learner is absent, or ill, or behind, or something is happening at home. The class keeps moving. A later concept depends on the missed one — and then another depends on that.

Week 1 · missed

Multiplying out brackets

Absent for two days. Nothing dramatic.

Week 3

Equations with brackets

Follows the class, gets the wrong answers, doesn’t know why.

Week 6

Rearranging formulas

Now two layers of the same missing step.

Today

Today’s lesson

Stuck, and out of explanations that aren’t about themselves.

Eventually the learner decides: “I’m just bad at math.”

The usual advice is “review.” Review what, exactly? Nobody has ever told them which step broke — only that they are behind.

Route

Recalculating

Step 1

A step gets missed.

Not a catastrophe, and usually not the learner’s decision. Two days away is enough.

Step 2

The damage spreads forward.

Everything downstream inherits the gap. The learner experiences this as “I’m bad at this topic,” because the actual break is weeks behind them.

Step 3

BACKTRACK recalculates.

It checks what they can still do, finds the one step that is actually load-bearing, and routes to that — not back to the start of the subject.

Step 4

The route reconnects.

The destination never moved. That is the difference between recovery and remediation: you are still going where you were going.

Google Maps doesn’t punish you for missing a turn. It recalculates.

Why doesn’t school?

A fair question

Why not just ask an AI tutor?

You should, when you know what to ask. AI tutors are genuinely good at explaining a topic you can name. That is not the situation BACKTRACK is built for.

A tutor answers

“Explain this topic to me.”

Excellent when the learner can already identify the topic, the confusion and the question. The learner is doing the diagnosis.

BACKTRACK answers

“Figure out why this topic stopped making sense.”

Built for the learner who says “I don’t even know why I stopped understanding.” The diagnosis is the product.

Gemini is a tutor. BACKTRACK is the GPS.

And during learning, use whatever helps — Khan Academy, a teacher, your notes, an AI tutor. Learn however you want. Prove what you know. BACKTRACK routes from what you can demonstrate afterwards, not from whether an explanation sounded convincing.

The mechanic

Keep me studying. Save me studying.

Most study apps keep you by threatening to take something away. BACKTRACK tries to keep you by visibly removing work you have proven you don’t need — and by making the thing you gained legible.

Shortcut found

You already know these steps.

Every question you answer well takes work off the route. Not points, not a badge — actual removed review. And it only happens because of something you did.

Division of labour

BACKTRACK finds the route. Khan Academy supports the learning.

These are two different jobs, and BACKTRACK is deliberately not trying to do the second one.

Current difficulty→ BACKTRACK check→ Khan learning & practice→ Fresh check→ Back to the destination→ Recalculate

A link click is not learning. BACKTRACK never marks a step repaired because someone opened a resource. It marks it repaired when the learner answers a fresh question they previously could not. This prototype does not assume a real-time Khan Academy API exists, and it does not claim one.

Two ways in

One destination for the class. A route for each learner.

BACKTRACK has to work for a teacher with thirty learners and for one person catching up alone at eleven at night.

School mode

The teacher sets the destination.

The teacher chooses or confirms today’s goal once. Thirty learners get thirty different routes to it, without the teacher building a rescue plan for each one.

  • Compact summary, not a dashboard to manage
  • Common blockers across the class
  • Which learners need a human, not another exercise

Self-study mode

Or you arrive on your own.

Through a topic selector, a shared link, a QR code, or a reel that caught you out. You pick the course, the topic and the goal, and BACKTRACK starts building a route.

  • A real chance to catch up on your own
  • The same logic as the classroom version

Views are not impact. Reach and learning are reported separately: qualified click, diagnostic start, diagnostic completion, route start, Khan practice initiation, return visit. A view count is not a learning outcome and will not be presented as one.

Status

Math first. Honest about the rest.

BACKTRACK is at concept and prototype stage. There is no pilot data yet, and this site will not invent any.

Stage

Concept / prototype built for KEIC 2026. No users, no partner schools, no results.

Proof domain

Mathematics first — the demo goes deep on equations with brackets, using Grade 8 as a reference scenario.

Proposed pilot

Approximately 80–120 formally evaluated learners across 2–3 partner-school cohorts, subject to school agreements and curriculum fit.●Planning target — not committed reach

Implementation window

1 November 2026 – 31 March 2027, if selected.

Product vision

Multiple math courses and grade levels, self-study and classroom use, and eventually other subjects with real prerequisite structure. None of that exists today.

The central claim we want to test

A learner could not do something relevant to their current lesson, received a targeted route, and later solved a fresh version of that task.

Everything above is a proposal. See the full evidence plan — what external research supports, what we are only hypothesising, and how we would measure it.
