How it works

Six moves, and only one of them is teaching.

BACKTRACK is a navigator, not a tutor. It works out where you are trying to get, what is actually in the way, and the smallest useful repair — then hands the teaching to Khan Academy.

01

Destination

You start from what you need now — today’s classroom lesson, an upcoming skill, a course topic, or whatever a short-form video just caught you out on. Not a placement level, and not the start of the subject.

02

Check

A short, honest check of what you can already do. Wrong answers are useful here; they are the only thing that tells BACKTRACK where to look. Nothing about it is graded.

03

Route

Everything you demonstrate is preserved and removed from the route. What remains is the prerequisite work that is genuinely load-bearing for the destination — usually far less than “review the whole unit”.

04

Learn on Khan Academy

The explanations, videos, worked examples and practice are Khan Academy’s. BACKTRACK does not reproduce them and does not try to compete with them. Opening a resource is never recorded as learning.

05

Prove

A fresh question — different numbers, different letters — decides whether the step is repaired. Learn however you want: Khan Academy, a teacher, your notes, an AI tutor. The check is what counts.

06

Recalculate

Then it keeps recalculating — if you improve, if you struggle, if you forget, if you disappear for two weeks, or if you turn out to know more than the route assumed.

The route, drawn

The destination never moves.

Everything else on the route is negotiable. That is what separates recovery from remediation: you are still going where you were going.

Honesty-first

Honesty gives you a better route.

At some checkpoints BACKTRACK asks how an answer felt. This never changes whether the answer was right — it changes what gets checked next. Cheating only buys you a route that assumes you know things you don’t.

Correct, and confident

Accelerate. The knowledge is preserved and the related review comes off the route.

Correct, but unsure

Still correct. But BACKTRACK keeps one check in reserve to verify it later, rather than dropping review on the strength of a guess.

Wrong, and “I forgot”

A refresher, not a re-teach. The material was there once.

Wrong, but confident

Possible misconception. BACKTRACK tests a contrasting case to tell a genuine rule error apart from a careless slip.

Wrong, and “never learned this”

Go straight to the missing foundation instead of spending ten diagnostic questions proving what you already told us.

One limit, stated plainly

Self-report is never treated as mastery, and BACKTRACK cannot reliably detect whether someone had help. That is why the decisions that matter rest on fresh work, and why formal pilot checks would need appropriate supervision.

What we are careful not to claim

BACKTRACK did not invent adaptive learning.

Prerequisite graphs, knowledge tracing, personalised courses, adaptive practice and AI tutoring all already exist, and some of them are very good. We are not claiming otherwise, and we are not claiming a mathematically guaranteed shortest path through anything.

The specific objective

Generic adaptive learning asks: what should this learner do next?

BACKTRACK asks: what is preventing this learner from understanding what they need now, and what is the smallest evidence-supported repair that reconnects them to it?

Ninety seconds is enough to see it.

No sign-in. The route responds to what you actually answer.
