Schools

One destination for the class. A route for each learner.

A teacher already knows some of the class is missing something. What they do not have is thirty diagnoses, thirty repair plans, and the hours to build them.

01

The teacher confirms today’s goal

Once, for the class. Usually the lesson they are teaching this week — not a separate intervention curriculum to plan and maintain.

02

Every learner gets their own route

Same destination, different prerequisites. A learner who already has the prerequisites goes almost straight there; a learner missing one step repairs that one step.

03

The learning happens on Khan Academy

Existing free content, in the school’s existing accounts. BACKTRACK’s job is deciding what is worth their time this period.

04

The teacher gets a summary, not a dashboard

Common blockers across the class, which learners a route repair will not be enough for, and whether the class is converging on today’s lesson. That is the whole teacher surface.

05

Humans handle what routing can’t

Some learners are not stuck on a prerequisite. They are stuck on attendance, eyesight, language, hunger or something happening at home. BACKTRACK’s job there is to say so quickly, not to keep serving exercises.

Proposed — not committed

The pilot we are asking to run.

Approximately 80–120 formally evaluated learners across 2–3 partner-school cohorts, subject to school agreements and curriculum fit.

A “120 learners / 3 schools” scenario is a planning target, not committed reach. No school has agreed to anything, and the final grade level and lesson destinations would be matched with partner schools rather than fixed in advance.

Delivery

The routine is co-designed, not imposed.

We are not going to hard-code “two twenty-minute sessions a week” into a product and then hand it to a school whose timetable does not work that way. Recurring access could be:

  • A remediation period
  • Advisory or homeroom
  • A computer-lab slot
  • Supervised after-class study
  • One or two short weekly slots
  • Structured homework with supervised verification

What the implementation actually needs

Repeated, meaningful use — with the constraints named.

The goal

Repeated meaningful Khan Academy use, enough exposure to evaluate learning, low teacher admin burden, and realistic device and connectivity access.

Devices & connectivity

Shared devices and scheduled connectivity windows where that is the honest situation. We do not claim fully offline Khan Academy functionality, because we have not verified it.

Paper activities

Useful for waiting periods when devices are shared — but they are not counted as Khan Academy usage, and will not be reported as such.

Scheduling

Exact dates are agreed with each partner. We are not publishing a January timetable for schools that have not been asked yet.

Learner data

The public demo collects no identifiable learner records. A formal pilot would need school agreement and appropriate data handling before any learner-level records exist.
