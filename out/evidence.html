Evidence

What we know. What we are testing.

These are different things, and this page keeps them apart. The research below supports the design decisions. It does not say anything about whether BACKTRACK works, because nobody has tried it yet.

No pilot results exist. There are no improvement graphs, percentage gains or learner testimonials on this site, because there is no data to make them from.

Established externally

What the research already supports.

Three findings we did not discover and are not claiming credit for. They are why BACKTRACK is shaped the way it is.

01

Teaching at the right level

Grouping and targeting instruction by a learner’s actual level, rather than by their grade, is one of the better-evidenced interventions in education. J-PAL reports the approach has reached tens of millions of children across India and Africa. BACKTRACK is an attempt to do that targeting per learner and per destination, rather than by reorganising a class.

povertyactionlab.org ↗ (opens in a new tab)

02

Retrieval and spacing

The IES practice guide Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning collects recommendations on spacing study over time and using retrieval practice. This is why BACKTRACK verifies with a fresh question rather than a repeat, and why the comeback check exists at all.

ies.ed.gov ↗ (opens in a new tab)

03

Autonomy and competence

Self-Determination Theory’s work on education argues motivation is better supported by autonomy, competence and relatedness than by pressure. This is the reason the Next Turn is optional, the stop button is real, and there is no streak to destroy.

selfdeterminationtheory.org ↗ (opens in a new tab)

Linking to these does not imply that any of these organisations knows about, supports or endorses BACKTRACK. They do not.

Unproven

What is only a hypothesis.

The specific claim BACKTRACK makes has not been tested by anyone, including us.

The product hypothesis

Visible route compression and capability unlocks can do the work that streaks and XP usually do — honestly.

That is the retention thesis, and it is the thing being tested. If it is wrong, adding dark patterns would produce better numbers and a worse product, so the numbers would stop meaning anything. Treat this as something to test, not as a proven effect.

We would be testing whether BACKTRACK improves

Review selection
Whether the work a learner is given is actually relevant to the destination they are stuck on.
Voluntary continuation
Whether learners take an optional next turn when it is genuinely optional.
Return behaviour
Whether learners come back after a gap, given that nothing was destroyed while they were away.
Purposeful Khan use
Whether Khan Academy time is spent on things connected to the current lesson.
Current-destination recovery
Whether learners can afterwards do the thing that originally stopped them.
Teacher diagnosis burden
Whether a teacher spends less time working out who is missing what.

Proposed measurement

How we would try to be wrong.

The central impact story is narrow and checkable: the learner could not do something relevant to their current lesson, received a targeted route, and afterwards solved a fresh version of that task.

Learning

  • Baseline destination score
  • Fresh post-route destination score
  • A delayed check after 7–14 days where feasible
  • Prerequisite gaps repaired
  • Reconnection to the destination

Khan engagement

  • Documented relevant Khan Academy practice
  • Repeated weeks of use
  • Return behaviour

Retention mechanics

  • Next Turn acceptance rate
  • Route-compression events
  • Voluntary continuation
  • Comeback after inactivity
  • Learner reports of pressure or frustration

Teacher & school

  • Setup time
  • Weekly admin burden
  • Destination reuse across classes
  • Support hours needed to replicate

Reported separately

Public reach — view to click, click to diagnostic, diagnostic to route, route to Khan practice, return visit — is reported apart from learning. Anyone can use the open public layer. A website visitor is not a documented learning recovery and will never be counted as one.

Two limits we would state up front

Baseline, return and delayed checks need fresh work under appropriate supervision to mean anything. And we cannot reliably detect remote assistance — from a person or from an AI.
